# Universal truths: what ten very different official sets all do

> **Editor's note.** This is one of the eight reports behind the study in `../README.md`, kept as its evidence. It is as its researcher wrote it, except for what this note lists. Points where the reports disagree with each other are dealt with in the README, not corrected here.
> Changed: the mention of the common brief and the names of folders on the researcher's computer were removed.
> One sentence below is wrong and is left as written: "none of the eight sets studied by the others". The other researchers studied 23 sets, and one of them, 31136 Exotic Parrot, is also in this report (the two readings are compared in the README).

Part of the sets study. I looked at ten official LEGO sets, chosen to be as unlike one
another as I could make them, and asked which building habits hold for **all** of them. I worked
independently and did not read the other researchers' reports.

All observations come from LEGO's own instruction PDFs, downloaded from LEGO's file server after finding
the links on `lego.com/service/buildinginstructions/<set>` with WebFetch. Each one is marked **[seen]**
with the PDF page numbers. Nothing here is [review], and nothing is [recalled] unless it says so. I give no
part numbers: none were checked, so parts are named by what they are.

That is nine themes (Creator appears twice, but as a bird and a ship), sizes from 85 to 2,083 parts, and
none of the eight sets studied by the others. In total I read 592 booklet pages.

**How I read them.** The PDFs could not be opened as they were, because no PDF page renderer is
installed. I wrote a short script that turns PDF pages into contact sheets of six pages per image, and
ran it with PyMuPDF from outside the project, so nothing was added to the project. The script and the
images were kept outside the download folders and outside the repository, and the images are for study
only.

For each set I read the first third of the build closely (for small sets, nearly all of it), then
sampled the middle, then read the end. The exception is the ship, where I read about half of the first
third.

## Notes, one line per observation

Step numbers are the booklet's own. "p" is the PDF page.

### 1. 75344 Microfighter (85 parts, 42 steps, about 2 parts per step)
- p5: the minifigure is built first.
- p6–10, steps 1–5: the base is made of plates. A long plate goes across a shorter one, a wedge plate covers that seam, and after the model is turned over, more plates go underneath and lock the layer from below.
- p9, step 4: two light-orange plates are added and then hidden under the skin. They are colour inside, not part of the look.
- p10, step 5 and p18, step 12: left- and right-hand wedges are shown together in the same step. They get no "2x".
- p15, step 10: the engine is built off to the side once and marked "2x". It is attached sideways (p16).
- p22–23, steps 16–17: brackets and clips are added so things can attach sideways. Orange call-out boxes zoom in on placements that would otherwise be hidden.
- p25–40, steps 19–34: the upper body, about a third of the parts, is built on its own and attached sideways to the brackets in step 35 (p41).
- p46, step 40: the canopy goes on a clip hinge. p47–48, steps 41–42: the guns are built "2x" and go on last. p49: a red-framed box shows the stud shooter working.
- The rotate icon appears at steps 3, 5, 32, 35 and 38.

### 2. 42147 Technic Dump Truck (177 parts, 90 steps, 2 per step)
- Booklet 1, p6: the first parts are an L-shaped beam and two axles: the front of the chassis. Everything is pins and beams; there are no studs.
- b1 p6–24: every axle or beam is shown with a full-size length guide ("1:1").
- b1 p16–19, steps 13–18: a frame block is built off to the side and joined to the front assembly.
- b1 p31–47: the whole grey chassis, with the gear and axle that tip the bed, is finished in booklet 1 before any coloured panel goes on. Small pin assemblies are marked "2x".
- Pins are colour-coded throughout: blue for pins with friction, tan for axle-pins.
- b2 p7–12: the yellow cab panels are built as small beam assemblies, stickers are applied in a sub-step, and the panels are pinned on.
- b2 p31–36: the blue bed starts from one big moulded panel and is pinned to a hinge at the back.
- b2 p49–50, step 90: the wheels are built "4x" and go on last. p51: a red box shows the bed tipping.

### 3. 40646 Daffodils (216 parts, 23 steps, about 9 per step)
- p4–7, steps 1–8: the **flower head** is built first, around a bar and a small hub. Two three-way bar connectors carry six petals. There is no base and no plate.
- p7 and p11: a red box with a tick and a cross shows which way round the petals go.
- p8, step 8: the yellow flower is marked "2x". Steps 9–16 repeat the build in white, drawn out in full because the colours differ.
- Hidden bars match their petals: tan under yellow, white under white. These are not contrasting inside colours.
- p12–15, steps 17–21: the stem is a chain of green tube sleeves on bar connectors, joined end to end with no overlap. One step places 13 parts (6 connectors and 7 tubes). Partway up, a cross-connector holds the leaves.
- p16, step 22: two long leaf plates clip on. The whole stem is marked "4x".
- p17, step 23: the heads are pushed onto the stems last.
- No stud connections carry the structure; it is bars, clips and tubes.

### 4. 76919 McLaren F1 (245 parts, 107 steps, about 2.3 per step)
- p4–5: the minifigure is built first.
- p6–10, steps 1–9: the chassis starts from a long dark frame plate with an opening. Plates are layered over it; yellow, blue and orange plates sit inside where they will not be seen.
- p13, step 15, and p32–36, steps 33–37: left and right parts are shown as two separate call-out boxes in one step, each with its own sticker. Stickers always go on in a sub-step before the part is placed.
- p15–17, steps 18–19: the nose block (bricks with side studs and holes) is built on its own and joined end-on.
- p29, step 30: an axle with a "1:1" guide.
- p49–50, steps 56–58: the front wing is built on its own and attached by an axle and pin. p67–71, steps 90–100: the rear wing is built on its own and attached near the end.
- p76, step 105: the halo, a thin bar, goes on late. p78, step 107: the wheels are built "4x" and go on last.

### 5. 31136 Exotic Parrot (253 parts, 108 steps, about 2.3 per step)
- p5–7, steps 1–4: the **display stand** comes first: two half-round plates, with slopes and a round plate over the joint between them.
- p8–9, steps 6–10: the branch is a sub-assembly of round bricks threaded on an axle (with a "1:1" guide), dropped into the stand.
- p11–18, steps 12–24: the flowers are built off to the side, some "2x", and attached sideways.
- p28, step 39: a block with an axle is built to take the bird's body.
- p30–36, steps 40–50: the bird's **body core** is made of white bricks with studs on their sides. The inside is a rainbow (red, pink, blue, orange) that none of the outside shows.
- p39–40, steps 58–59: the side skins are plates topped with curved slopes. They are built "2x" and fixed sideways to the side studs.
- p49–51, steps 76–81: the tail is a plate assembly that clips on. p52–54, steps 82–87: the second wing is drawn out in full because it is a mirror image.
- p61–65, steps 102–108: the head is built on its own and goes on last.

### 6. 21256 Frog House (400 parts, 83 steps, about 4.8 per step; bag 1 groups several small builds per step)
- p5–12, bag 1: the minifigures and furniture (furnace, cake, crafting table, bed, chest) are built first, each as a mini build with numbered sub-steps.
- p13–18, bag 2, steps 1–8: the **base is several plates**, tied together by bricks laid across them and by plates added over every seam.
- p30–32, steps 18–24: a corner module is built "2x" off to the side and joined to the base.
- p33, step 25: two plates are added across the seam where the modules meet.
- p34–36, steps 26–28: stilts of 1×1 round and square bricks are built "2x" and placed.
- p49–54, steps 40–45: walls of plain bricks on a large tan floor plate. Tan wall pieces are built "2x" as small stacks.
- p74–78, steps 67–71: the roof (the frog's head) is built separately on its own large plate and lifts off.
- p85–87, steps 81–83: a garden module is built on its own and attached to the side last.
- Almost every stud points up; I did not see sideways building in the pages I read.

### 7. 71785 Titan Mech (794 parts, 265 steps, about 3 per step)
- p7–18, bag 1: the minifigures and a small cart are built first. The cart's launcher is shown working in a red box (p18).
- p20, bag 2: the pilot minifigure.
- p21–28, steps 1–24: the **torso core** comes first. It starts from a 4×6 plate and grows a ring of white bricks with studs on their sides. A red bracket and a red brick sit inside.
- p29–33, steps 25–39: the hip assembly goes on with click hinges. The yellow "ear" icon at step 37 tells the builder to listen for the click.
- p34: the cockpit opens on a hinge, shown with a yellow arrow.
- p55, steps 90–109: a leg is drawn once and marked "2x": the legs are built identical, not mirrored.
- p70–72, steps 129–136: the feet are built "2x" identical, but wedge plates are split into left (step 131) and right (step 133).
- p72, step 136: the feet go on after the legs. **The mech is built from the torso downwards**, not from the ground up.
- p85–87, steps 172–178: an arm is built and attached at the shoulder with a ball joint.
- p121–132, steps 249–265: the sword and other weapons, each threaded on an axle, are built last. The final steps place what the mech holds.

### 8. 60414 Fire Station with Fire Truck (843 parts, roughly 300 steps over 4 booklets)
- b1 p5–6: the minifigures come first. b1 p9–14: a small drone, shown working in a red box.
- b1 p15–18, steps 1–5: the truck chassis. A large black plate, long blue plates that stick out past its end, then a second large plate under them. **The long plates bridge the joint between the two big plates.** Blue, green and tan plates are hidden inside.
- b1 p19–20, step 6: a sub-assembly is joined end-on.
- b1 p28–30, steps 14–16: bricks with side studs are added, and side panels are built "4x" and fixed sideways.
- b1 p64–65, step 52: a side locker is built "2x". b1 p115–123, steps 113–123: the ladder is built on its own and goes on a turntable last (p124–125: red box with the ladder working).
- b2 p2–6: one 16×16 baseplate starts the building. b2 p7, step 3: a brick with holes and pins at the edge to join the next module.
- b3 p43, step 40: two 13-long Technic beams are pinned upright as pillars to tie the walls together. That is Technic used to stiffen an ordinary building.
- b4 p13–18: the upper floor is built on its own plate. b4 p49–52, steps 47–50: the fire pole goes in last.

### 9. 31109 Pirate Ship (about 350 steps)
- b1 p5–13: the minifigures, cannons ("2x", with a red box showing them firing) and a shark are built first.
- b1 p14–18, bag 2, steps 1–10: the midship begins on a large tan plate with a second plate beside it. Long dark plates run all the way round the edge, then plates are laid across the joints.
- b1 p19–23: bricks with studs on all four sides, for building sideways. Yellow and blue marker bricks are hidden inside.
- b1 p24–35, bag 3: the stern is built on its own plate and joined end-on at step 55. b1 p36–48, bag 4: the bow is a separate module.
- b1 p55–57, steps 104 and 106: matching left and right hull panels are shown as separate call-outs. b1 p58, step 107: long plates go across the whole deck.
- b1 p97–102: the captain's cabin walls are built as flat panels and then attached.
- b1 p157–162: the masts are bars and axles. The sails are big flat plate assemblies clipped to a spar.
- b1 p193–198, steps 341–350: the bowsprit sail, spars and the figurehead parrot go on last.

### 10. 10318 Concorde (2,083 parts, 547 steps, about 3.8 per step)
- p11–19, steps 1–17: the wing core is **built sideways**, standing on its edge. Long white plates are stacked so their joints never line up; each step's new parts are outlined in red. The first parts in the middle are red and blue. A text note on p14 says the wing was built on its side to keep it thin and still capture the swept shape.
- p20–23, steps 18–28: a Technic gearbox for the landing gear is built on its own and set into the middle of the wing.
- p28–36, steps 36–45: the wing is laid flat. Mechanism housings are built around the gearbox.
- p61–66: the other half of the wing is again a long stack of plates with joints offset, with a red-and-blue block in the centre.
- p91–96, steps 127–140: fuselage sections are hollow shells with the drive shafts inside, built off to the side and set onto the wing. p91 has a tick-and-cross check.
- p121–126, steps 193–205: rear wing section. Orange Technic beams are fitted inside the white shape as a stiffening spine.
- p181–183, steps 293–294: the roof sections lift off to show the cabin.
- p265–269, steps 489–490: the flaps are built "4x". Left- and right-hand flap sequences are each drawn in full.
- p277–279, steps 513–523: the tail fin is built on its own and goes on almost last.
- p280–288, steps 524–547: the display stand comes last, as its own build.

## 2. Truths about order

**Tested sequence:** first a base as long as the model, then what carries load, then the skin, then what
sticks out, then what is fragile.

- **As stated, it does not hold.** "First a base as long as the model" fails three times: the flower
  (starts with a head on a hub), the mech (starts with the torso) and the Concorde (starts with a wing
  stood on its edge). See C10.
- **With the first step reworded to "the core", it holds 10 of 10** for the order parts are *attached*
  (C2). "Core" means the base, the spine or the central block, whichever the subject has.
- **The order parts are *built* is 9 of 10.** Small separate items (minifigures, furniture, cannons, the
  cart, the flower heads) are often built first and put on late.
- **Smaller order truths:**
  - **Fragile or held things come last. 10 of 10.** Guns, canopy, wheels, halo, flower heads, bird's
    head, sword, ladder, fire pole, sails, figurehead, tail fin and stand.
  - **Every flat or moulded base comes before what stands on it. 10 of 10** for the bases that exist.
    The stand is a separate case: the parrot's comes first, the Concorde's last.
  - **Inside before outside: 10 of 10 in assembly order.** Interiors come before the roof: frog house,
    station, ship's cabin, Concorde cabin. Body cores come before their skins: parrot, mech, microfighter.
- **Not true:** bottom-up (C11) and front-to-back. The truck, the F1 car and the dump truck build front
  first. The ship and the frog house build the middle first. The Concorde builds its centre and works
  outwards along the wing.

| Strength habit | Holds | Breaks or can't tell | Label |

| Things built on their own are joined with several connections | Clearly true for structural joins (body panels, modules, fuselage sections) | Moving and display joints are single: a flower head on one bar, the mech's arm on one ball, each gun on one bar | depends: fixed joins use several, joins meant to move or come off use one |

**When does a pin-and-beam core appear?** In these ten, only in the Technic set. Technic parts turned up
in brick-built models of every size, but in different jobs:

- as an axle spine (the parrot's branch, the mech's sword, the masts)
- as a mechanism (the Concorde's landing gear)
- as stiffening (the station's pillars, the Concorde's orange beams)
- as joints (the mech)

None of the stud-built sets, not even the 2,083-part Concorde, had a pin-and-beam frame as its core. The
Concorde's core is plates stacked so their joints are offset, with Technic set *inside* it.

|---|---|

| A step never adds more than a few *kinds* of part. A bigger group becomes a framed box with numbered sub-steps (1, 2, 3…) and an arrow to where it goes | 10 of 10 |
| Large counts in one step happen only for identical parts (six petals, seven tube sleeves, eight bricks in a row) | 10 of 10 where a large step occurs |
| Identical units: drawn once, "2x" or "4x" | 10 of 10 |
| Mirror units: both drawn | 6 of 6 where present |
| A rotate icon whenever the model must be turned | 10 of 10 |
| A placement that would be hidden gets a zoomed call-out with an arrow; the builder is never asked to put a part where they cannot see it | 10 of 10 |
| A "1:1" full-size guide for every axle or beam whose length matters | 8 of 8 sets with such parts; 40646 can't tell (the parts list marks the length but I saw no guide box in the steps) |

| New parts outlined in red each step | 1 (10318, the adult Icons style): depends on the audience |
| The last page shows the finished model | 10 of 10 |

**What the builder is never asked to do** (10 of 10 in the pages I read):

- place a part without seeing where it goes
- add two different repeated units in one step
- build a mirror copy from a "2x"
- work out a length by eye
- hold a loose assembly against the model without an arrow showing exactly where it lands

## 5. What looked universal and was not

These matter as much as the truths.

1. **"Start with a base as long as the model."** Broken by the **Daffodils** (40646), which begin with a
   flower head on a bar and a hub; the **Titan Mech** (71785), which begins with the torso; and the
   **Concorde** (10318), whose first part is a wing core built standing on its edge.
2. **"Build from the bottom up."** Broken by the **Titan Mech**, which goes torso, hips, legs and then
   feet, and by the **Concorde**, built on its side.
3. **"Finish the core, then put the skin on."** Broken by the **Microfighter** (outer slopes at step 6 of
   42) and the **McLaren** (stickered outer slopes at step 15). Small sets put skin and core on together;
   only the big or Technic ones finish the core first.
4. **"Hidden parts are a contrasting colour."** Broken by the **Daffodils**, whose hidden bars match
   their petals: tan under yellow, white under white.
5. **"Every seam is bridged / everything joins by studs."** Broken by the **Daffodils**: stem segments
   are joined end to end on connectors with nothing overlapping, and no studs carry load anywhere in the
   model.
6. **"Small steps of 1 to 3 parts."** Broken by the **Daffodils**: one step adds 13 parts and the average
   is about 9. The real rule is "few *kinds* per step; many only when they are identical".
7. **"Symmetric halves are built as mirror pairs."** Broken by the **Titan Mech**: legs and feet are
   designed to be *identical* and built "2x", with only a few wedges handed.
8. **"Large models have a Technic pin-and-beam core."** Broken by the **Pirate Ship** (1,264 parts, a
   plate-and-brick hull) and the **Concorde** (2,083 parts, a stack of offset plates with Technic only
   inside it). In these ten, only the Technic set has a pin-and-beam core.
9. **"Minifigures are built first."** True only for the sets that have them (6 of 6). The Technic,
   botanical, Creator animal and Icons sets have none.
10. **"Moving parts join with at least two connections."** Broken by the **Titan Mech** (arm on one ball
    joint) and the **Daffodils** (head on one bar). Joints meant to move or come off use one joint.

## 6. Ten rules for a builder

Strongest first. The size range is the parts count of the sets each rule was seen in.

1. **Build the core first, the part everything else will hang on (a base, a frame, a spine or a central
   block), and attach everything else to it, never the core to them.** 10 of 10, 85–2,083 parts.
2. **Attach in this order: core, then body and skin, then appendages (wheels, wings, limbs, roof, side
   modules), and last whatever sticks out or is held (weapons, sails, heads, antennae, ladders).**
   10 of 10, 85–2,083 parts. Build order is 9 of 10; small loose items may be made early and fitted late.
3. **Build any repeated unit once and reuse it; where a shape is symmetric, design the copies to be
   identical rather than mirrored.** Repeats 10 of 10, 85–2,083; the identical-copies habit was seen in
   4 sets, 253–843 parts.
4. **Make every large or complicated region a separate sub-assembly that is complete on its own, then
   join it to the core; above about 800 parts, build the whole model as several such modules.**
   Sub-assemblies 10 of 10, 85–2,083; modules 4 of 4 at 794–2,083.
5. **Cover every joint in a layer that carries load with a longer part above or below it: lay long
   plates across the joint between two plates, and join modules with plates across the seam.** 9 of 10,
   85–2,083 parts; the exception (Daffodils, 216) has no studs at all.
6. **Give every long thin part a stiff spine (an axle, a bar, a long beam or a stack of long plates) and
   hang the shape on it.** 8 of 8 sets that have long thin parts, 177–2,083 parts.
7. **Put each moving part on one joint made for moving (clip hinge, ball, click hinge, axle, turntable)
   on a solid assembly, and fix everything that should not move with several studs.** 10 of 10 have such
   joints, 85–2,083 parts. That fixed joins use several studs is "usual", not counted.
8. **When a part must be left- or right-handed, build both hands explicitly; never reuse a copy.** 6 of 6
   sets that have mirror pairs, 85–2,083 parts (4 sets had no pair I could see).
9. **For any shaped surface, build sideways from the core (bricks with studs on their sides, brackets,
   clips) as well as on top.** 7 of 7 stud-built sets with a shaped skin, 85–2,083 parts. Absent by
   nature in Technic and the stem-built plant; not seen in the block-world Minecraft set.
10. **Keep each step to a few kinds of part and a few placements, and place many parts at once only when
    they are identical.** 10 of 10 for "few kinds", 85–2,083 parts; 9 of 10 for "about 2 to 5 parts per
    step on average". This is about writing the instructions, but it also limits how much can change
    between two checks.

## 7. What ten sets cannot tell us

- **Why, and how strong.** Instructions show what was done, not why, and not what would have failed. I
  could not test a single joint or count connections.
- **Brick bond.** "No two joints lined up above each other" could only be checked in three sets; the
  drawings do not show enough of the inside for the rest.
- **Hidden colours.** Whether they have any meaning beyond helping the builder see the new part. My guess
  is that they don't, but that is a guess, not something I saw.
- **Whole categories missing:**
  - a pure sculpture with no function: a helmet, a bust or BrickHeadz
  - a flat mosaic or art panel, which may have no "core" in this sense
  - Duplo
  - a large Technic supercar, to see whether "core first" means the gearbox or the frame there
  - an older set from before about 2015, before today's instruction style
- **Rules 3, 5, 7 and 8 (the weakest).** Rule 3's identical-copies part was seen in only 4 sets. Rule 5
  has a known exception, and a mosaic or a sculpture could break it again. Rule 7's "several studs for
  fixed parts" was not counted. Rule 8 was seen in only 6.
- **Further sets that would test them** (set numbers and names are [recalled], not checked):
  - **a helmet such as 75304 Darth Vader Helmet:** a side-stud core and skin all round; does rule 1 still hold?
  - **a mosaic such as 31203 World Map:** no core at all; does rule 1 survive?
  - **10281 Bonsai Tree:** a plant with a pot base; rule 1 against the Daffodils
  - **42143 Ferrari Daytona SP3:** whether a big Technic model attaches parts in the order of rule 2, and where the core is
  - **a BrickHeadz figure:** very small, built on a block; rules 4 and 9 at about 100 parts
  - **a large creature such as 31129 Majestic Tiger or a T. rex skeleton:** whether big creatures switch to a Technic core, as big machines might
  - **any older set, before 2010:** whether rules 3, 8 and 10 are modern instruction style or how sets have always been designed
