# Style guide for the builder: designed, not dated

Draft, 20 November 2026. No AI build has used it yet. It teaches techniques; never rebuild or imitate a set.

Evidence marks: **D** LEGO designers say it. **B** expert builders say it. **S** seen in official sets, old against new. **T** tested on our checker. **I** our inference. Two marks is strong; **I** alone is a guess.

## (a) Before you build: write the art direction

Write these eight lines first. Build to them. Change them only on purpose.

1. **Scale:** how many studs wide, and what that width lets you show that the next size down would not. Take the largest scale the parts asked for will pay for, never below the smallest at which the subject's signature shapes can be made from real parts. Spend the parts on shape and finish, not on hidden filling or an interior nobody sees. [D B I]
2. **Colours:** one body colour (about 70%), one second colour (20%), one accent (under 10%), plus black or grey for mechanics and glass. Say where each goes. [B S]
3. **Pattern:** how the colours divide the body (stacked zones, a sweep, a band a third of the way along). Decide it before the first part. [B S]
4. **Shapes:** the three to five shapes that make this subject, and the part that makes each. [D]
5. **Sideways:** which faces are built as flat panels and pressed on (flanks, nose, face, sign). [D S]
6. **Thin:** which members must be a bar, a tile or a plate seen on edge. [S I]
7. **Studs:** where they stay and what they stand for (rivets, fur, a walkway). Otherwise none. [D]
8. **Glass and stance:** one tint and how it is framed; how low the model sits and on what. [D S]

## (b) Rules of finish

1. **Cap every upward face** with curved slopes or tiles: terraced studs are the first sign of an old set. Tiles 3070b 3069b 63864 2431 6636 4162 3068b 87079; curved slopes 11477 15068 50950 61678 93273 88930 93606. [S D] The tool cap tiles every free stud of a top in one call, leaving the studs you keep. [T]
2. **Keep studs only where they mean something**, in a row or a patch, never scattered. [D S]
3. **Skin the sides.** Build each flank flat on a plate, turn it, press it on side studs: no stack of plate edges on show. Side-stud bricks 87087 11211 30414 22885 4070; brackets 99780 99781 99207 44728 36840 36841. [D S B] Put them in the core facing out; the tool skin then presses plates on their studs and tiles the plates' faces in one call. fill is for hidden cores, never for a face seen from outside. [T]
4. **Round the underside too.** Inverted curved slopes (24201 32803 13547) make the body tuck under; a 45-degree staircase does not. [S B]
5. **Taper in plan.** Make nose and tail narrower than the middle with wedge plates: 24299/24307, 43722b/43723b, 41769b/41770b, 65426/65429. [S]
6. **One livery.** Each colour has one job. Carry a division unbroken across parts: a row of tiles of the stripe's colour on a skin, a sideways band, or pairs of wedge plates and a triangular tile (35787) or a tile with an angled end (22385) for a sweep. Never a stripe as a stack of plate courses: that is the flat slab of an old set; a plate course only where it is hidden or holds the structure. Put a band off-centre. [B S]
7. **Prefer deep and muted colours** to bright red, blue and yellow on white, which is the 1980s palette: dark blue 272, dark red 320, dark green 288, sand blue 379, sand green 378, dark azure 321, dark orange 484, medium nougat 84, dark tan 28, greys 71 72. Use a primary as the accent, or when the subject is that colour. [S; writers' opinion]
8. **Thin things thin.** Struts, legs, rails and aerials are bars (87994, 30374, 4095), a tile or plate on edge, or a lever (4592 with 4593). If the scale cannot show it thin, leave it out: small official planes do. [S I] A clip on a handle holds at any angle (60897, 61252 on 2540, 48336, 60478), but only what carries nothing (struts, rails, aerials, mirrors, blades); a leg the model stands on is fixed by studs, a pin, or a bar seated in a hollow. A bar in a bar tube (23443, 11090) or a clip holds at any angle about its line, and the checker tests both for fit; a thick U-clip (4085c) is only counted as joined, so prefer C-clips (60897, 15712, 61252). The tool clip puts a clip on a bar or handle at any angle and says where the part points; strut puts a bar between two handles, an 11090 on each. [T]
9. **No bare Technic holes, pin ends or undersides on show.** Turn the plain side out or cover it. [I]
10. **Glass is one tint, set into the body**, narrower than it, with pillars in the body colour or a dark one. Take the rake from a moulded screen (57783, 30841, 18973) or a clear panel on a hinge (60581, 87552 on 3937 with 3938: the lid-hinge module, put in with place_module). Use clear 47 over an interior, or trans-black 10375 with none. Never trans-brown 40, never a stack of tinted bricks. [S]
11. **Gather detail, leave calm between.** One or two detail colours; every detail says what it is. Small parts: 98138 6141 85861 54200 2412b 4589 4740 25269 24246. [B S]
12. **Hide a gap or make it a line.** Back an opening with black; line seams up where the real thing has a panel line. [S B]
13. **Make angles with parts made for them:** wedge plates, hinge halves, a clip on a handle, a part turned on one stud. Lengths that close exactly: 3 studs by 10 plates, 8 studs by 5 bricks. [B T]

## (c) By kind of subject

**Aircraft.** What dates it, and what to do:
1. A slab fuselage of stacked plates: build a 2-stud core with sideways skins, curved slopes on top, inverted curves below.
2. A stair-stepped tail: one long curved part a flank (77180/77182) or a wedge-plate taper.
3. White with a red or blue waist stripe: three stacked zones and one accent, or a sweep up the fin.
4. A light-blue or brown box of glass under a roof plate: a small dark canopy faired in, or clear glass in frames over an interior.
5. Thick struts and bare wheels: bars or blades, fairings of curved slopes, or neither.

Smallest: sides and belly can be rounded from 4 studs wide (2-stud core, 1-stud skins: on brackets, wall 4 + plate 8 + tile 8 = 20 LDU a side; on side-stud bricks plate and tile make 16, 3.6 studs wide). An interior needs 6. Under 4, shape the top only. [S, few sets] For a light aircraft, about 300 parts pay for 4 wide and about 600 for 6 wide with an interior behind clear glass. [I, the Cessna critique]

**Car.**
1. Terraced bonnet and roof: curved slopes and tiles.
2. Tall and narrow, the whole tyre showing: low and wide, an arch over each wheel (18974 98282 50745), the body down around it.
3. A clear cabin as wide as the body: a tinted glasshouse narrower than the body.
4. One width nose to tail: narrower ends, flanks sideways with a crease.
5. A vertical wall at sill and bumper: tuck under; one body colour over black.

Smallest: 8 studs wide for true proportions; 6 is a caricature; 4 is a toy. [D]

**Creature or character.**
1. A coloured stud as an eye: a pupil with a glint (black 85861 with a white bar end in its stud, or black 98138 on a white round plate), in a socket, under a brow.
2. A stepped body: curved caps; studs only as fur or scales.
3. A thin box: rounded masses (cheeks, hips) and a larger head.
4. Structure colour showing: coat, its dark shade, a pale belly, one accent.
5. Blunt ends: claws (15070 49668), a horn on a bar or peg (53451), nose, tongue, ears.

Smallest: 4 studs wide, about 150 parts. [S]

**Building.**
1. One flat wall: base, body and crown, with a cornice that sticks out.
2. Holes for windows: inset frames, sills, surrounds; two window types.
3. White and red: two or three wall colours, trim, one bold accent.
4. A studded baseplate: tiled pavement, lamp, awning, plant.
5. An open back and a roof that just stops: a closed volume, a finished roofline, texture in patches (98283).

Smallest: about 12 studs wide, two storeys. [S]

**Object.**
1. Studded faces: tile every face.
2. A blank surface: rows of tiles whose gaps are the grooves.
3. One flat colour for a material: sideways layers in close shades.
4. Thick edges: a hairline from plate edges (32028 4510).
5. Sharp corners: curved slopes; lettering only where a printed part exists.

Smallest: about 8 by 8 studs. [S, one set]

## (d) A critique to run before finishing

Look at your pictures and answer yes or no. A "no" means rebuild that region, not add parts.

1. Does the outline alone say what it is?
2. Is each signature shape there, with the detail gathered at it and calm surface between?
3. Is every stud on show one I chose?
4. Are the sides and upward faces free of plate stacks and stair-steps?
5. Three colours or fewer, plus neutrals, each doing one job?
6. Does each colour division run unbroken across the parts, off-centre?
7. Is everything thin that is thin on the real thing?
8. From all three views, no Technic hole, pin end or underside showing?
9. Does the glass read as glass: one tint, framed, set in?
10. Does it sit as the real thing sits: level, low, on its wheels, feet or base?
