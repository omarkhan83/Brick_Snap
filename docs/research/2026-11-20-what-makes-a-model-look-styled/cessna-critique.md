# The Cessna 172 of 20 November, read as an art director would

The model: 206 parts, 36-stud span, 29 studs long, fuselage 4 studs wide; checker clean. Judged from its
three-view sheet, its file and the builder's notes, against `style-guide.md`. The real aircraft's sizes
below (span about 11 m, length about 8.3 m, cabin about 1 m wide, main wheels about 2.5 m apart) are
general knowledge, not checked in this study.

## Verdict

It is a correct small plane with a good wing and an old body. Five things carry the "old set" look, in
order of how much they cost:

1. **The flanks.** The fuselage is plates stacked flat (the builder: "stacked plate layers rather than
   brick walls, so the two stripes could run nose to tail"). Every plate edge shows as a line, the
   sides are vertical and the belly is flat. This is the one thing every modern official model with a
   shaped body avoids, and the largest single difference between the two official Beetles of 2008 and
   2016 (guide rules 1, 3, 4).
2. **The livery.** White with a bright blue and a bright red line at the waist, blue tips and a red
   spinner is the palette and the pattern of the 1987 and 1990 Town aircraft (a white body, a red or
   black plate line at the waist, primaries only). A waist line as a plate course is not itself dated
   (2020 and 2024 City planes still use one); these colours and a line that never leaves the
   horizontal are (rules 6, 7).
3. **The glass.** Bricks and 45-degree slopes in colour 40 make a brown block. In our library 40 is
   Trans Brown; the neutral smoke of modern sets is Trans Black, 10375, which is not on the builder's
   colour list. There are no pillars, no frame, and the block is as wide as the body (rule 10).
4. **The struts.** Technic beams with a row of holes where the real thing is a thin blade (rules 8, 9).
5. **The undercarriage.** Bare wheels on pin plates, no legs to speak of, no fairings. The moulded fin
   belongs here too: one part cannot carry a livery.

Not a fault: the wing. Curved slopes along the leading edge and a tiled top are more finish than the
official propeller planes under about 900 parts that we looked at have (nearly all use bare studded
plates with a square front edge).
Keep it. Keep the navigation lights, the cowl inlets on side studs, the turning propeller and the
9-stud wheel track.

## The scale I would choose

- **About 300 parts: stay 4 studs wide, change how the 4 are made.** A 2-stud core of side-stud bricks
  with a 1-stud skin pressed on each side is the narrowest body whose sides and belly can be rounded
  (two Creator planes and the 203-part official Vega of 2021 do it at this width). Span 42 studs, length
  31 or 32: the real ratio of span to cabin width is a little over 10. No interior: at this width it
  cannot be seen, and the Vega's designers left theirs out too. The four seats and dash go.
- **About 600 parts: 6 studs wide.** Span about 62, length about 47. Six wide is where an interior
  fits behind clear glass, where doors can be drawn, and where the 8-studs-by-5-bricks strut triangle
  (37 degrees) is close to the real strut's slope and length.
- Not 5 wide. Odd widths put the centre line on a stud and fight every pair of parts; the Speed
  Champions team is reported to have tried 7 wide and found it confusing to build (second-hand).

## A livery

Not the example's dark blue, ochre and white. Proposal, **"graphite sweep"**:

- **White (15)** above: roof, upper flanks, wing, tailplane. About two thirds of what is seen.
- **Dark bluish grey (72)** below: belly and the lower third of the flank from the nose to the back
  of the cabin, then rising in one diagonal behind the cabin so that the tail cone and the whole fin
  are grey. The division is level, low and long, then sweeps: it is never at the middle.
- **Orange (25)** as the one accent, under a tenth: a line one plate thick along the division from the
  nose to the back of the cabin, the wingtips, the fin cap.
- **Trans black (10375) glass in black (0) frames.** Legs and propeller light bluish grey (71) or black.

Every part needed exists in these three colours, which matters for a model meant to be built.
Brighter alternative: dark azure (321) for the grey, bright light orange (191) for the line. Heritage
alternative: dark red (320) below, a tan (19) line. No registration: the parts index has no printed
parts, and lettering built from bricks needs a far larger flank.

How it is carried: the level line is the edge of one orange plate in the core, showing between the
white panel above and the grey panel below (a line one plate thick seen edge-on, as official sets
draw tiger stripes and trim lines). The sweep is drawn on the sideways tail panel with pairs of wedge
plates, one white and one grey to each rectangle (43722b/43723b, 41769b/41770b), or triangular tiles
(35787).

## The brief the builder should have written

1. Scale: 4 wide, 42 span, forced by the narrowest roundable body; no interior.
2. Colours: white 15, dark bluish grey 72, orange 25; trans black 10375 and black for glass.
3. Pattern: grey below a low level line, sweeping up behind the cabin to take the fin.
4. Shapes: the high straight wing on one strut a side; the raked screen running into the wing's
   leading edge, with the cut-down rear window; three wheels on splayed legs in fairings; the tail
   cone tapering to a swept fin; the flat-fronted cowl with two round inlets.
5. Sideways: both flanks, the nose face, the fin (wedge plates on edge).
6. Thin: struts, main legs, nose leg, propeller, aerials, trailing edges.
7. Studs: none on the body; one row along the flap and aileron line as a hinge.
8. Glass and stance: dark band set into the flank, narrower than the roof; level, belly 3 studs up.

## What to change, in order of effect

### Within about 300 parts

1. **Rebuild the body round a 2-stud core** (87087, 11211, 30414, brackets), with flank panels pressed
   on, curved slopes lengthwise along the roof and tail top (11477, 15068, 88930) and inverted curved
   slopes for the belly corners (24201, 32803, 13547). About 125 parts with the nose. This one change
   removes the plate lines, rounds the section and gives the livery a surface.
2. **Apply the livery** as above. No extra parts: it is the colour of the panels.
3. **Glass:** a moulded raked screen in trans black (57783 or 30841), a flush side band of trans-black
   tiles or plates on side studs between black pillars, a sloped rear pane. About 18 parts.
4. **Struts as bars:** a clip plate (60897) under the wing and at the sill, each gripping the handle
   of a Bar Tube with Handle (23443), a Bar 4L (30374) between the two tubes. Handles 5 studs apart
   across and 3 bricks apart in height give 36 degrees and seat the bar 8 LDU deep at each end. Today's
   checker passes exactly this (tested). Ten parts for both sides.
5. **Legs and fairings:** main legs as a 1 x 3 or 1 x 4 tile on a hinge or a wedge plate, splayed;
   each wheel under a fairing of curved slopes (93273 or 50950 over, 24201 under), white above and
   grey below; a bar for the nose leg. About 24 parts.
6. **The fin from wedge plates on edge**, grey with an orange cap, with a small fillet ahead of it;
   tailplane tiled. About 25 parts.
7. **Nose:** curved slopes sideways on all four sides of the cowl so the front is round, not square;
   spinner grey or orange, not red.
8. **Wing:** lengthen to 42; orange tips with round-corner parts (30357, 27925); a row of studs or
   grille tiles (2412b) at the flap line. About 80 parts, mostly the ones there now.
9. **Details, few:** one exhaust stub, two aerials (4592 with 4593), a landing light. Stop there.

### Within about 600 parts (6 wide)

Everything above, and:

1. **Clear glass (47) over an interior:** two front seats and a bench in medium nougat (84) or dark
   tan (28), a black panel with round-tile dials, two control yokes. The screen from a moulded 6-wide
   part (18973, 3384) or clear panels (60581, 87552) raked on hinges between dark pillars.
2. **A rounder section:** a 4-stud core with skins, so the flank can curve in at the roof and the belly
   over two rows, and the tail cone can taper in plan and in height at once.
3. **Doors as drawn panels**, their seams lined up; a step on each leg.
4. **Flaps and ailerons as separate tiled surfaces** with a gap, set a few degrees down on hinge
   plates; an elevator and rudder the same way.
5. **Blade struts:** a strut of 1-wide plates, tiled, with a clip plate (60897) under each end, on
   handles (48336) 8 studs apart across and 5 bricks apart in height: the two clips are then exactly
   200 LDU apart, at 37 degrees (tested, passes). A blade, thin from the front, is what the real
   strut is.
6. **A two-colour sweep** with wedge-plate pairs, the orange line following the grey up the fin.
7. **A wing with a section:** three plates thick at a quarter chord, thinning to a tile at the
   trailing edge, with root fairings.

## What the builder cannot do today

- **Bars in the tube of 11090:** the checker calls the bar loose (see `findings.md`, section 4). Use
  23443 until that is fixed.
- **Trans black and most colours newer than 2000** are not in the colour list the builder is given.
- **600 parts:** the form stops at 300 and one session.
- **Angles by hand:** every tilted part is a matrix the builder works out itself. It got the struts
  right on 20 November; a tool that places a clip on a handle at an angle would make this routine.
