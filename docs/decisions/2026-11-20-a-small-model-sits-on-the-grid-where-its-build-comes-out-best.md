# A small model sits on the grid where its build comes out best, not always centred
- status: accepted
- by: Alec-42, 2026-11-20

Whether a round section about three studs across comes out 2 x 2 or 3 x 3 depends on where its axis
falls on the grid, and until now that was decided by the parity of the model's width. A model of up to
16 studs is now also tried half a stud over in x, in y and in both; each seating is built, and another
seating is kept only when its outline is clearly closer (a point on the mean, a point more for every
tenth more parts, no view worse by more than a point), it is no less sturdy by the shared measure, and
it joins no more of the model's separate sections. The project remembers the seating; a project made
before, a model over 16 studs, and a side that is a whole number of studs are untouched. On the samples
the bottle moves at 8, 12 and 16 studs and the knight, pawn and teapot at 12; the mean outline rises two
points at 8 and 12 studs. It costs up to about four builds when a small project is made.

Rejected, each built and measured first (see `docs/tasks/where-a-model-sits-what-is-left.md`): filling
a thin column out in the make-buildable pass (it fattened square legs and fused legs a stud apart), and
filling each section for the best area match in the conversion (a box no longer stayed a box). The fill
rule of 17 November, a cell is filled when the model fills most of it, stands unchanged.
