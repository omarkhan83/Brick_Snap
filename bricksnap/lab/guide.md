# How set designers build: a guide for an AI builder

**Draft.** No AI build has used this guide yet; each part of it stays only where a test shows it helps.
It is written from 32 official sets' instruction booklets, from counts over 5,935 sets' parts lists
(Rebrickable catalogue, November 2026) and 42 official models' building order, and from what LEGO's
designers and experienced builders have published (see [the study](README.md)). It teaches techniques:
never rebuild or imitate a particular set.

Words: a *side-stud brick* has studs on its sides. A *bracket* is a plate with a second plate standing at
its edge. A *panel* is a flat group of parts built on a plate, turned on its side and pressed onto side
studs. A *beam* is a studless bar of holes; *pins* join beams: a friction pin holds, a smooth pin turns.

## Rules for any subject

1. **Build the core first**: the one assembly everything else attaches to: a base, a block of side-stud
   bricks, a box of walls, a spine or a frame. Attach things to it, never it to them. [10 of 10] Beams
   and pins are rarely it: under 350 parts at most 3 sets in 100 hold ten beams; at 500 to 750, 17 in
   100, and about twice that in two action themes. [5,711 sets]
2. **Attach in this order**: core; body and skin; limbs, wheels, wings, roof; last, whatever sticks out,
   is fragile or is held. [10 of 10] Wheels typically go on 86% of the way through. [26 models]
3. **Build apart, then join.** Make each repeated, shaped or fiddly region a small unit, finish it, then
   attach it. [10 of 10] Two thirds of a model's parts sit in such units of 6 to 10 parts.
   [36 models]
4. **Build a repeated unit once and copy it.** Make left and right the same unit turned round, or
   build one side and mirror it. [10 of 10]
5. **One joint for each moving part**, made for moving (wheel pin, clip on a bar, hinge, turntable,
   axle), on something solid. Hold everything else by several studs. [10 of 10] A thin member
   that carries nothing (a strut, rail, aerial, mirror or blade) may hang on one clip on a bar
   or handle; a leg the model stands on is fixed by studs, a pin, or a bar seated in a hollow.
   [style study, 2026-11-20]
6. **Cover every seam.** Where two parts meet in a layer that carries weight, lay a longer part across
   them in the next layer, and prefer one larger part to several small ones. [9 of 10; LEGO's model
   builders, 1998; J. Berard of LEGO, 2006]
7. **Build shaped faces sideways.** Put side-stud bricks or brackets in the core, build the face flat as
   a panel, press it on. Round it with curved slopes, not a staircase of bricks. [7 of 7 sets with a
   shaped skin] A quarter of a model's studded parts face sideways: a tenth in the first third of the
   build, a third in the last; hardly any upside down. [36 models]
8. **Give anything long and thin a stiff spine**: an axle, a bar, a long plate or a beam. [8 of 8]
9. **Use only joints the parts were made for.** Force nothing, wedge nothing between studs, push pins
   fully in, set click hinges on a click, hang nothing on friction alone. [Berard, 2006] A clip
   gripping a bar or handle holds by its shape: it is a joint made for that. [style study,
   2026-11-20]
10. **Work in small steps.** An official step adds 2 parts (median), more only when they are identical.
    Place a few parts, then check. [36 models; 10 of 10]
11. **Keep weight off joints.** A long or heavy part on a clip, hinge or ball joint droops: shorten or
    lighten it, or carry it on a pin or axle. Give a model that cannot stand a base. [LEGO designers,
    2019]
12. **Many kinds of part, a few of each.** A set of 100 to 150 parts has about 45 different parts in 13
    colours, most used about twice; one of 250 to 400 has about 89 in 18. [435 and 807 sets] Hidden
    parts may be any colour. [8 of 10]

## Playbooks

### Vehicle, 100 parts
[one 94-part car; habits shared by 4 of 4 vehicles]

- **Core:** one base as long as the vehicle: a one-piece car base, or a plate with a wheel-pin plate
  (4600) under each axle. The wheel sets the scale. A body on it: fill with sides, then skin, then
  cap.
- **Strong:** run a long plate along the top of each side.
- **Outside:** build one side unit twice and turn the second round. Stand a 1 x 4 side-stud brick (30414)
  across each end of the floor; build lights and grille flat on a plate and press them on. Curved slopes
  and tiles on bonnet and boot (cap tiles a top in one call); an arch part over each wheel.
- **Moves:** wheels on their pins. The roof rests on two studs only, so it lifts off.
- **Order:** base and wheel mounts first; sides before end faces; roof near the end; wheels last.
- **Not:** hinged doors unless asked.

### Vehicle, 400 parts
[three vehicles of 261 to 301 parts]

- **Core:** a spine: one plate as long as the vehicle (4282), wheel-pin plates under each axle, a second
  long plate shifted so no seams line up. Stand side-stud bricks at nose and tail for the end faces,
  then lay one wide plate across the spine as the floor.
- **Strong:** cap every wall with a plate longer than any brick under it.
- **Outside:** brick walls on the floor. Sills, tanks, lights, grille and rear face are panels on
  brackets or side-stud bricks. A wedge plate on edge makes an angled side. Bumpers hang from clip plates.
- **Moves:** wheels on pins; a reel or coupling on its own pin through a brick with a hole; a lift-off
  roof.
- **Apart, in order:** spine; side pairs; end mounts; floor; body; lockers, tanks, grille and bumper;
  roof; wheels.

### Aircraft, 100 parts
[seen in 7 aircraft sets of 1987 to 2025; the core in 2 Creator planes, 2020 and 2025]

- **Core:** a spine 2 studs wide of side-stud bricks (87087, 11211, 30414) on a long plate, with a
  skin one stud thick pressed on each side (quickly: fill with sides, then skin, then cap). The
  wing sets the scale.
- **Strong:** the wing is one long course of plates passing over or through the body, locked by a
  second layer of plates that overlaps the joint.
- **Outside:** flanks as panels on the side studs (skin makes each in one call); curved slopes on
  top, inverted curved slopes under the belly and tail; the screen one moulded part; the fin on jumper plates on the centre line.
- **Moves:** the propeller on a pin or a bar; the wheels on a gear plate under the core.
- **Order:** spine; wing course and its lock; skins; tail; gear under the core; struts, aerials and
  propeller last.
- **Not:** a box of stacked plates; struts or wheel fairings the scale cannot show thin.

### Aircraft, 400 parts
[seen in 4 sets: 2 Creator planes, 2020 and 2025; a 203-part tribute plane, 2021; a 2012 biplane]

- **Core:** a spine 2 to 4 studs wide of side-stud bricks and brackets on two long plates whose
  seams do not line up; 6 studs wide overall leaves room for an interior.
- **Strong:** the wing passes through the body as one long course of plates, locked above and
  below; a long plate runs along each side of the spine.
- **Outside:** each flank a panel carrying its livery line (skin, on brackets or side-stud bricks); nose and tail tapered in plan with
  wedge plates; inverted curved slopes for a rising belly; the fin of wedge plates on edge, on
  jumper plates on the centre line; the screen moulded, or clear panels on hinges.
- **Moves:** the propeller on a pin; wheels on gear plates or pins under the core; flaps and
  rudder on hinge plates only when asked.
- **Order:** spine; wing and its lock; floor and interior; flanks; nose; tail; gear; struts,
  aerials and propeller last.
- **Not:** Technic beams with holes as struts; a box of stacked plates.

### Hollow thing with an inside, 100 parts
[scaled down: the smallest hollow building read had 400 parts]

- Choose how the inside is seen: an open back or a lid.
- **Core:** a plate the size of the footprint, then one course of bricks tracing it, with a gap for the
  door.
- **Floor:** tiles, leaving two to four single studs for furniture: two or three units of 3 to 6 parts,
  placed while the walls are low.
- **Walls:** one stud thick, courses overlapping, corners alternating which wall runs through. Frames
  for door and window. A long plate over every opening.
- **Lid:** one plate over all the wall tops, held by their studs alone, so it lifts off. For a pitched
  roof, step slopes up the end walls.
- **Not:** a filled block or thick walls. A building too small for a figure is built solid: a
  side-stud core with panels.

### Hollow thing with an inside, 400 parts
[four buildings of 400 to 843 parts and one far larger]

- **Base:** long plates side by side, a crossing layer, a large plate on top; then one course of bricks
  round the footprint and a tiled floor with single studs left for furniture.
- **Furniture:** 4 to 8 units a room, 4 to 12 parts each, placed while the walls are low.
- **Walls:** one stud thick, long bricks in overlapping courses, corners woven or of corner bricks
  (2357). A side-stud brick (87087) wherever a lamp or sign goes.
- **Strong:** end each storey with an unbroken course of long bricks over every frame and pillar, or
  with plates over all the wall tops.
- **Upper storey or lid:** a separate unit of two crossed plate layers, ceiling colour underneath,
  resting on the wall-top studs so it lifts off.
- **Roof:** flat panels on two hinge plates each, or a flat lid edged with slopes.

### Creature, character or sculpture, 100 parts
[three figures of about 120 to 200 parts]

- A box with rounded faces, not a staircase chasing the true curve. The eye, a round tile (98138), sets
  the scale.
- **Body:** a stack about 4 studs square, with a side-stud brick or bracket wherever a paw, arm or chest
  panel attaches, capped with a plate.
- **Head core:** eight 1 x 1 side-stud bricks (87087, 26604) ringed round a plain one, two layers,
  capped with a plate; for a bigger head, twelve taller ones (22885) round a 2 x 2 column. Such bricks
  and brackets are about one part in six. [196 sets]
- **Face:** a panel on a 3 x 4 plate: two eyes, a nose on a jumper plate, a slope for the muzzle.
- **Sides and top:** mirrored panels of curved slopes or tiles; small slopes soften the corners.
- **Order:** body from the feet up (ears and legs on studs, the tail on a clip and bar), head core,
  sides and top, face.

### Creature, character or sculpture, 400 parts
[four sets of 253 to 794 parts]

- **Core:** a box on a 6 x 6 plate: columns of side-stud bricks at the corners, facing out; brackets
  facing the other sides; a plate closing each of two storeys. The middle may stay hollow.
- **A long animal:** the middle a closed ring (long plates below and above, side-stud bricks at the
  ends); chest and hips as separate blocks, joined end to end by two or three small ball joints.
- **Skin:** one panel a side: a base plate, plates in shrinking outlines for the curve, curved slopes
  only where the eye falls. Close corners with small slopes or a strip of plates.
- **Head:** its own smaller core; the face its own panel.
- **Apart:** limbs, wings, ears, tail: light ones on clips, posed ones on click hinges or ball joints,
  anything heavy or turning on a pin or axle.
- **Stand:** if it cannot stand, a base with a beam or axle up into the core.

### Plant, 100 parts
[nine potted plants in one set; four stems in another]

- **Pot:** a square plate, a wall one or two bricks high round its edge, a rim of small panels; inside,
  plates and a socket.
- **Spine:** a bar or short axle in the socket, threaded with two or three round plates that have bar
  handles, small round bricks between.
- **Leaves:** one leaf, a curved slope or wedge on a clip plate (61252), built 8 to 12 times and clipped
  to the handles in tiers, each tier turned half a step, lower tiers tilted further out.
- **Top:** a brick with studs on four sides (4733) and a centre piece.
- **A single flower instead:** head first, petals clipped round a small hub of bars; the stem an axle,
  or sleeves on bar connectors; push the head on last.
- **Mix:** in plant sets, pins, axles and connectors, clips and bars, and leaf and flower parts are each
  a sixth of the parts; plain bricks and plates, under a tenth. [53 sets]

### Plant, 400 parts
[a 608-part potted plant; a 756-part bunch of loose stems]

- **Pot:** square, a box of bricks. Round, a drum: stacked round plates tied together by upright beams
  pinned up the outside, carrying many copies of one narrow panel sideways.
- **Stake:** two long axles (50450 is 32 studs) stood in the pot, tied with cross links, the main stem
  threaded through.
- **Stems:** thin and straight, one long axle; thick, tube connectors on short axles; curved, a chain of
  fixed-angle connectors: more pieces, longer arc.
- **Leaves:** a plate carrying large curved wedges, a clip at its foot, hung on bars at the pot's rim at
  different angles.
- **Flowers:** one flower built 5 to 8 times: a small hub, petals on clip plates (car bonnets, wings,
  claws and horns serve), a centre piece; each plugs onto a stem by an axle.
- **Order:** pot, stake and stems, leaves, flowers. Loose stems share no core.

### Machine or framed model, 100 parts
[two machines of 143 and 177 parts]

- Decide what moves and build that first: an axle through a short beam, with its gear.
- **Core:** close a compact block round it: two beams beside the axle joined by an open frame on
  friction pins (2780), two pins at every joint, five to seven holes long. No long rails.
- **Pivots:** a tower is two bent beams or two flat frames side by side with axles through both.
- **Moves:** the arm hangs on smooth pins (3673), driven from the block's axle; a worm gear (shaped
  like a screw) holds it in place.
- **Outside:** almost none: a thin beam or one panel a side, each on two pins. Wheels on smooth pins at
  the block's corners, last.
- **Fixing:** a part that must not turn gets two pins in two holes, or a pin and an axle. One pin makes
  a hinge.
- **Not:** studs in the structure; hinges for angles (use bent beams).

### Machine or framed model, 400 parts
[a 544-part car]

- Use a frame only for a drive, steering or suspension, or a length near 40 studs; otherwise use the
  vehicle playbook.
- Build the drive or axle unit first; it sets the distance between axles.
- **Rails:** each is two long beams side by side, pinned every two to four holes, ends staggered. Odd
  lengths (11, 13, 15 holes) have a centre hole. Join the rails at both ends with a closed rectangle: an
  open frame (64179) or cross beams. L-shaped beams stand up from the rails to carry seats and sides.
- **Outside:** panels on two pins each, far apart. Wheels last.
- **Not:** triangles, except where the frame changes level or width.

## Parts by job

Numbers are LDraw part numbers found with the project's part search.

| Job | Kinds of part |
|---|---|
| Core that takes panels | Side-stud bricks: 1 x 1 with one (87087), two (26604) or four side studs (4733); 1 x 2 with two (11211); taller 1 x 2 with four (22885); 1 x 4 (30414); headlight brick (4070). Brackets: 99780, 99781, 99207, 44728 |
| Core that rolls | Plate with two wheel pins (4600); long plate (4282); one-piece car base; wheel arch |
| Half-stud shift | Jumper plates (3794b, 34103) |
| Curve and detail | Curved slopes 2 x 1 (11477), 2 x 2 (15068), 4 x 1 (61678); low slopes (85984, 54200); wedge plates; round tile (98138); grille tile (2412b) |
| Swinging joint | Clips (61252, 63868, 15712) on bars or handles (60478, 26047); hinge brick (3937 with 3938); swivel hinge plate (2429 with 2430) |
| Posed joint | Ball (14417) in socket (14418); click hinges |
| Turning joint | Smooth pin (3673); axle pin (3749); brick with a hole (3700); turntable (3680 with 3679) |
| Frame | Beams (32316 has 5 holes, 32278 has 15); open frame (64179); L-shaped beam (32526); friction pins (2780, 6558); bricks with holes (3700, 3703) |

**Sizes that fit.** Two studs sideways equal five plates of height: make a sideways panel an even number
of studs across, or use a bracket for the odd half plate. [B. Ward, 2022]
